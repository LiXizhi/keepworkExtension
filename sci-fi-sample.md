# The Last Cartographer

The signal arrived at 03:14 ship-time, wrapped in static that tasted like rust.

"Mira? Can you hear me?"

Dr. Mira Osei pulled the headset closer, her eyes on the stars crawling past the porthole of the *Tern*. Ten years she had sailed the dark alone, mapping the uncharted drift between the Cygnus corridor and the quiet edge of nowhere. Ten years without another voice.

"I can hear you," she whispered, afraid the words would dissolve the moment.

"Thank God. This is relay buoy 44-G. We've been pinging every cartographic unit in the sector. You're the only one who picked up."

"Picked up what?"

A pause. Static rattled. Then: "We found something. A door. On a moon nobody ever named. It's been opening and closing for a thousand years, and nobody knows what's behind it. The Academy wants it mapped before the next silence. You're the closest."

Mira looked at her empty coffee mug, at the crumpled star-charts taped to every wall — her life's work, all of it guesses and patience. A door. Not a star, not a nebula, but a door.

"Send me the coordinates."

The moon was smaller than her ship, gray and cratered like an old tooth. She landed in a basin where the craters formed a perfect spiral, and there it stood: a ring of black metal, twenty meters tall, standing upright in the dust as if it had grown there. It hummed. The hum was low, almost friendly.

Her instruments went crazy — no, not crazy. They started reading something new. A second set of coordinates, written in the same spiral, blooming across the moon's surface like handwriting.

"Buoy? You see this?"

"Copy that. Mira... the door opened."

She turned. The ring was now a doorway, and beyond it there was no darkness. There was a library — shelves of light stretching into infinity, each shelf holding a map. Not maps of stars. Maps of *doors*. Every door, on every world, that had ever been opened or left closed.

A figure waited inside, made of the same soft glow.

"You took your time," it said. "We've been waiting for a cartographer. Someone who knows that a map is only half the journey — the other half is the courage to step through."

Mira thought of ten years of loneliness, of rust-tasting static, of an empty mug. She stepped through.

The door closed behind her. On the moon, the spiral faded, and the dust settled like nothing had happened. Somewhere, a new star appeared in the charts of the next lonely cartographer, and a new signal began to ping.
